Thursday morning, Jensen Huang lost his job. By Thursday night, the richest man on Earth had offered him a new one. Gamblers were betting real money on where he'd land. And 1,600 Nvidia employees were begging for one thing: bring him back.

Quick recap. Two affairs, one alleged spy, and a board that said "effective immediately." Today's question is different: who gets him next?
It started with one post. Zero Hedge wrote: "Elon, please hire Jensen." Elon replied in five words: "That would be an honor."
Tech split in two. Tim Sweeney called it a sad day for "all humanity." Lex Fridman compared it to Apple firing Steve Jobs. But investor Sarah Guo said it best: greatness doesn't guarantee goodness.

On Polymarket, Elon's SpaceX and xAI started near 27%, then dropped to about 10. Why? Every name on that board is Nvidia's customer, supplier, or rival.
Elon first. Perfect on paper: he wants his own chip factory. But Jensen still owns over 800 million Nvidia shares. Every chip he helps Elon build is one Nvidia doesn't sell. And two men who always need the final word? Same building? Same company?
Next, TSMC. Back in 2013, founder Morris Chang tried to hire Jensen as his successor. Jensen said no. Today it sits at about 5%.
AMD's Lisa Su is a distant relative of Jensen's. Imagine that family dinner.
Then SoftBank. Masayoshi Son sold his Nvidia stake twice. Now he'd have to buy the man himself. And SoftBank owns ARM, whose boss Nvidia's board reportedly approached for Jensen's old job. Musical chairs, with trillion-dollar chairs.
Or he starts over, like 1993, three engineers in a Denny's booth. This time, with $100 billion.

But here's the strangest bet. Will Jensen rejoin Nvidia? Seven percent by year-end. Thirty-one percent by the end of next year. That's higher than every rival.
The reason is the spy question. Trump said: if she was a spy, he was right to go. If not, "perhaps it would be different." Nothing has been proven, China denies it, and Jensen has admitted the affair and apologized.

Why should you care? If you own an S&P 500 fund, a big slice of it is Nvidia. This is your retirement.
Watch two dates: Nvidia's first earnings without him, and December 1st, when Congress questions him.
Two weeks ago, nobody could picture Nvidia without Jensen. Now the betting money says neither picture lasts.
"Any offers, Mr. Huang?" "Elon, TSMC, SoftBank, a few governments." "What are you looking for?" "My old desk."
